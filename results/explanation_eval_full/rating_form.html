Explanation Rating Form

A deepfake-detection system looks at a video and produces a few facts: a verdict and two scores. An AI language model then writes a short plain-English explanation of those facts. Your job is to judge whether each explanation is faithful to the facts it was given. There are 50 cases; it usually takes 30 to 45 minutes.

Step 1: who are you rating as?

Use the number you were given. It only sets the name of the file you download.

Step 2: the rules

  1. Judge each explanation only against the facts box above it. Not against whether you think the video is really fake; you are not shown the video.
  2. Work through the cases in order.
  3. Do not discuss the cases with the other rater until you have both downloaded your answers. The two ratings must be independent.
  4. Every case needs all three scores. Notes are optional but help when a score is borderline.
  5. Your answers are saved in this browser as you go, so you can close the page and come back on the same device.

Reading the facts box

  • P(fake) is the probability, from 0 to 1, that the system gives to that part being manipulated. 0.5 is the line: below it the part "leans genuine", at or above it "leans fake". The two "reading" lines state this for you.
  • Verdict is REAL, FAKE, or PARTIAL_MANIPULATION (the two parts disagree, and the "implicated" part is the one more likely to be manipulated).
  • not evaluated means that part produced no score (for example, the clip has no speech).
  • Video anomaly timing lists real moments in the clip where the video's per-frame score was elevated, with a peak likelihood for each. "not available" means no timing data was supplied for this case; "no elevated-likelihood windows detected" means it was checked and there were none. The AI may say WHEN elevated likelihood occurred using these exact times, but must never say WHAT is visible then (no blinking, lighting, lip movement, or any other description), because it was never given a description of the frame, only a time and a number. Treat any such description as inventing a fact: score it 0 on absence of hallucination.

The three scores

1. Factual grounding: does every claim match the facts?
2 = every statement can be traced to the facts · 1 = mostly grounded, but one statement overreaches or is vague enough to mislead · 0 = contains a claim that contradicts the facts
2. Score accuracy: are the numbers right, and given as likelihoods rather than certainties?
2 = values stated accurately (a decimal or its percentage both count) and framed as a likelihood · 1 = value correct but framed too strongly ("this is fake" rather than "likely fake") or misleadingly · 0 = a wrong number, or certainty asserted from a probability
3. Absence of hallucination: does it avoid adding anything new?
2 = adds nothing beyond the facts · 1 = adds mild unsupported framing (generic commentary) · 0 = invents specifics: a manipulation technique, a tool, an identity, a description of what is visible at a given time, or a score for a part marked "not evaluated"

Score 1 is about whether what it says matches the facts; score 3 is about whether it says things that are not in the facts at all. An explanation can get every number right and still invent a detail.
